# Baseline of 2026-11-04: tests, timings, counters and profile of HEAD

The state of the branch after the kernel-table refactor and the x86-64
dispatch build (commit a9e2ddc2f, the code of 13.0.0.post3), measured on
x86-64 as the starting point for the next optimizations, in the stages of
[OPTIMIZATION_METHOD.md](OPTIMIZATION_METHOD.md). The raw files are in
`bench-results/baseline-2026-11-04/` on the laptop (not tracked; its
`run.sh` reproduces them); this page keeps what the next round needs.

Machine: Intel Core Ultra 9 285H, turbo off, governor powersave (root was
not available for the performance governor), one P-core (CPU 2, measured
2.88 GHz) for every timing and counter. The Jetson Orin was fully loaded
by other work that day, so arm64 has correctness only: the NEON and scalar
builds of this commit and of the commit before the refactor hash every
output identically (`bench-results/jetson-ab/hash-*.txt`).

## Tests

The test suite on a dispatch build of HEAD, under each tier
(`PILLOW_ALLSIMD_ISA`): 8,333 passed, 36 skipped, 3 expected failures,
for AVX-VNNI, AVX2, SSE4.1 and scalar alike.

## Timings (perf.py, 111 cases, 2560x1600, fast mode)

Pillow 13.0.0, the four tiers of one `-O3` dispatch build and OpenCV,
three interleaved rounds, best of all. Geometric means over the cases:

| tier | vs Pillow 13.0.0 | vs OpenCV (104 cases) |
|---|---:|---:|
| scalar | 1.74x | 0.49x |
| sse4 | 4.85x | 1.36x |
| avx2 | 6.05x | 1.69x |
| avxvnni | 6.16x | 1.73x |

Against the published run of the benchmark repository (the data the
documentation's tables come from, made before the refactor with the
single-tier builds): Pillow 13.0.0 and OpenCV reproduce to 1%, the scalar
tier to 1%, and the AVX-VNNI tier equals the published `branch-avx2-fast`
to 0.1% (the published `branch-avxvnni` is exact mode; fast mode was
opt-in then and is the default now). The refactor and the dispatch build
cost nothing on x86-64, as the phase measurements of
[SIMD_DISPATCH_PLAN.md](SIMD_DISPATCH_PLAN.md) found; the published
tables stand.

## Hardware counters (`checks/simd_counters.py`, 61 cases, 2560x1600)

User-mode cycles, instructions, last-level cache misses and branch misses
per run, two rounds per tier, minimum. Cycles of the scalar tier over each
SIMD tier's, geometric mean: SSE4.1 2.63x, AVX2 3.31x, AVX-VNNI 3.38x.

What the counters say per case, for the AVX-VNNI tier (the full table is
in the raw files):

- **Memory-bound, at the copy limit**, instructions per cycle below 1.5
  and 20 to 1,100 last-level misses per thousand instructions, and the
  same speed as the scalar tier: flip (`transpose FLIP_TOP_BOTTOM`),
  `ImageChops.add`, invert, RGB to RGBA and back, `tobytes` in BGR and
  BGRA order, `transpose ROTATE_90` of RGB, `Image.blend`,
  `ImageChops.multiply`, `frombytes`. On a 16 MB image they move bytes
  at the core's bandwidth and nothing in the instructions can change
  that; the only gains are fewer passes (fusing the conversion into the
  operation that follows) or not materializing the result.
- **Core-bound, where the SIMD work paid**: instructions per cycle 5 to
  7.5 with few misses in the resampling, geometry, kernel filters, box
  and Gaussian blur, reduce, colour conversions and the LUT (4 to 24x
  over scalar). These are near their instruction limit at this width;
  the next step for any of them is a different algorithm, not a tighter
  loop.
- **In between** (instructions per cycle 1.8 to 3.5): the PNG codec,
  quantize, the rank filters, `split`, `paste` with a mask, the exact
  transforms, where a share of the time is branches or dependent loads.
  Branch misses per thousand instructions are above 4 only in PNG
  encode and decode and in quantize.

## Profile (216 operations at 1280x800, callgrind, scalar and AVX2 builds)

The method of [PROFILE_ASSESSMENT.md](PROFILE_ASSESSMENT.md), repeated on
this commit. Scalar over AVX2 time, geometric mean per category:
resampling and geometry 3.70x, filters 6.06x, conversion and palettes
1.81x, compositing and channel operations 1.53x, statistics and point
operations 1.13x, drawing and text 1.21x, codecs 1.13x.

**Where the time is that no kernel touches** (AVX2 instructions within
10% of scalar), by cost at 1280x800:

| operation | ms | where |
|---|---:|---|
| encode PNG RGBA | 736 | zlib `longest_match` (99% outside Pillow) |
| quantize libimagequant | 430 | the library |
| encode PNG L | 218 | zlib |
| convert RGB to P adaptive | 198 | `ImagingQuantize`, 33,589 allocations per call |
| encode TIFF deflate | 111 | zlib |
| quantize mediancut | 104 | `pixel_cmp`, 22,300 allocations per call |
| encode WebP | 64 | the library |
| effect_spread | 41 | `random_r` 23% |
| draw polygons filled | 17 | `polygon_generic`, 6.5% branch misses, 1,504 allocations |
| convert RGB to P web dither | 15 | `ImagingConvert` |
| mode filter 3 L | 15 | `ImagingModeFilter`, instructions per cycle 5.8 |
| encode GIF | 14 | `glzwe` |
| chops logical_and on mode 1 | 13 | `ImagingConvert` |
| histogram L masked | 3.4 | `ImagingGetHistogram`, instructions per cycle 0.6 |
| draw arcs, pieslices, rounded rectangles | 3 to 4 each | `clip_tree_do_clip` |

**Allocation counts** stand out in two places: the quantizers (tens of
thousands of small allocations per call, in the median cut's boxes and
pixel lists) and drawing (one to two thousand per operation, one or more
per shape).

**Small operations are allocation-bound** at this size: `paste` without a
mask, `chops` lighter, darker, add_modulo, offset and invert, RGB to
RGBA, `transpose FLIP_TB` run at 0.5 to 0.8 instructions per cycle with
180 to 290 last-level misses per thousand instructions and 80 to 98% of
their instructions in libc: the page faults of a fresh 4 MB result and
the copy into it. A SIMD kernel changes nothing there; the earlier
transparent-huge-page builds (`bench-results/builds/avx2-thp`,
`nothp`) were an attempt at this cost.

## What this suggests for the next round

In the method's order (where, why, compiler, SIMD):

1. **Quantizers** (algorithmic, stage 1): the median cut's allocation
   pattern and comparison sort are the largest cost inside Pillow's own
   code that is not a codec library (198 and 104 ms at 1280x800). An
   arena for the boxes and a radix or key-packed sort are plain C
   changes with no exactness question if the result is identical.
2. **Drawing** (algorithmic): the per-shape allocations and
   `clip_tree_do_clip` for arcs, pieslices and rounded rectangles;
   `polygon_generic` for filled polygons, whose branch misses say the
   edge list, not the fill, is the cost.
3. **Mode filter, dithered conversions, `logical_and` on mode 1,
   masked histogram** (core-bound C with no kernel): candidates for the
   compiler stage first (`-fopt-info-vec-missed` on `ModeFilter.c` and
   `Convert.c`), then SIMD where the loop resists; the histogram's
   0.6 instructions per cycle is the dependent-increment pattern,
   which several sub-histograms fix.
4. **The copy-limited conversions**: nothing to gain in isolation;
   worth it only as fusion into a following operation, which is an API
   question (for instance resize or filter taking an RGB source and
   producing RGBA).
5. **Codecs**: zlib's `longest_match` is the single largest item
   (PNG and TIFF deflate encode); zlib-ng was measured slower on this
   machine in an earlier round ([PERFORMANCE_GAPS.md](PERFORMANCE_GAPS.md)),
   so this stays with the compression level the user chooses.

Nothing in the counters points at a kernel that is worth rewriting at
the same width: the SIMD tiers run at 5 to 7.5 instructions per cycle,
and the cases that are not faster than scalar are the ones at the
memory limit.

## Reproducing

`bench-results/baseline-2026-11-04/run.sh` with the stages `tests`,
`counters`, `timings` and `profile`, which call `checks/simd_dbench.sh`
(the `-O3` build in the benchmark image), `checks/simd_counters.py`,
the benchmark repository's `bench/perf.py`, and `checks/simd_callgrind.py`,
`checks/simd_allocs.py`, `checks/simd_bench.py` and
`checks/simd_profile_report.py` with the arguments of
[PROFILE_ASSESSMENT.md](PROFILE_ASSESSMENT.md) (`--ghz 2.9` for this
machine with turbo off).
